For 3.1 Research Design, I'd keep it short — probably ~300–450 words. Its job is to give the reader the experimental logic of Chapter 3, not explain any component in detail.

Your literature review has already established the rationale: develop against BioRED, freeze the resulting extraction system, then test it on independently assembled contemporary literature to investigate generalisation. Research Design should state how you operationalise that idea.

3.1 Research Design
Paragraph 1 — Overall design

State what kind of study this is and its high-level structure.

Something conceptually like:

This study adopts an experimental pipeline-development design in which an LLM-based biomedical information extraction system is iteratively developed against an established benchmark corpus before being frozen and evaluated on independently sampled contemporary biomedical literature.

Then briefly identify the major stages:

BioRED development → final pipeline selection → contemporary evaluation → KG construction/evaluation

A simple methodology overview figure would work particularly well here.

Do not re-explain:

why biomedical IE is necessary;
why LLMs are useful;
what distribution shift is;
why BioRED is broader than ChemProt/GAD.

Chapter 2 has already done all of that.

Paragraph 2 — Development vs evaluation separation

This is probably the most important methodological principle to establish here.

Explain that BioRED has distinct train/development/test data and that your iterative EPI development is separated from final evaluation.

At a high level:

EPIs are developed and compared using the designated development data.
Prompt/schema changes are informed by development performance/error analysis.
A final EPI is selected.
The final configuration is then frozen.
Subsequent evaluation does not involve further prompt refinement.

Don't describe each EPI, prompts, metrics, few-shot construction, etc. Those belong in 3.5 Pipeline Development.

The point here is experimental validity:

development data informs decisions; final evaluation data does not.

Paragraph 3 — Two evaluation settings

This is where I'd explicitly connect the design to your research questions.

You effectively have:

Benchmark evaluation:
How well does the final extraction system perform against established BioRED ground truth?

Generalisation evaluation:
How does that same unchanged system perform against independently sampled contemporary literature?

This is crucial because the comparison is only meaningful if you don't alter the extraction system after seeing contemporary-corpus performance.

Again, don't re-explain why benchmark performance might fail to generalise. You spent the end of Chapter 2 establishing exactly that.

One sentence such as:

This separation allows benchmark performance and performance on unseen contemporary literature to be compared without further adaptation of the extraction system.

is enough.

Paragraph 4 — KG stage

Briefly establish where KG construction fits:

After extraction and normalisation, the resulting entities and relations are assembled into the contemporary biomedical KG, followed by graph-level evaluation.

Don't explain:

triples;
nodes/edges;
why NEN matters;
ontology integration;
graph metrics.

Those belong in 3.6 and 3.7, and you've already established their conceptual rationale in Chapter 2.

What 3.1 should not contain

This is the easiest way to avoid repetition. 3.1 should answer only:

What did the overall experiment do, in what order, and how were development and evaluation kept separate?

It should not answer how any individual stage works.

So I'd avoid detailed discussion of:

BioRED composition → 3.2
contemporary corpus acquisition → 3.2
model/API/settings → 3.4
prompts/JSON schema → 3.4
individual EPIs → 3.5
error-analysis procedure → 3.5
NEN implementation → 3.4/3.6
KG assembly → 3.6
P/R/F1, strict/relaxed/cosine → 3.7
KG metrics → 3.7
costs → 3.8
The ideal shape

I'd aim for something roughly this compact:

P1: overall experimental design + stages
P2: controlled iterative development on BioRED
P3: freeze final system → benchmark/contemporary evaluation
P4: construct/evaluate KG + point reader toward following sections

The key difference from Chapter 2 is:

Literature review: Why is benchmark-to-contemporary generalisation worth investigating?
3.1 Research Design: What experimental structure did I use to investigate it?

If you maintain that distinction, there should be very little genuine repetition, even though naturally some terms such as BioRED, contemporary corpus and frozen pipeline will appear again.
