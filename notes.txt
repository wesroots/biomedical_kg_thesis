3.2.1 BioRED Benchmark Corpus
Paragraph 1 — Dataset identification and acquisition

Very briefly identify the dataset:

BioRED benchmark corpus.
600 PubMed abstracts.
manually annotated entities and relations.
source/version and how you obtained it, if relevant.
state that the official dataset partitions were retained.

You only need perhaps 2–3 sentences of descriptive material.

You could explicitly signpost backwards:

BioRED, described in Section 2.4.2, was used as the benchmark corpus for extraction-system development and evaluation.

That gives you permission not to repeat the literature review.

Paragraph 2 — Dataset partitions

This is much more important methodologically.

State the exact:

training set size;
development set size;
test set size;
role assigned to each.

Then explain your separation:

Train→development resources/few-shot examples
Development→EPI development and selection
Test→final held-out benchmark evaluation

assuming that accurately reflects your final procedure.

This is where you establish data leakage prevention. Don't explain the whole EPI development process—that's 3.5—but clearly state which partitions were allowed to influence development decisions.

Paragraph 3 — Data actually used by the extraction system

Describe the relevant fields/annotations you loaded from BioRED.

For example:

PMID/document identifier;
abstract text;
entity annotations;
entity types;
entity spans/text;
normalised identifiers;
relation annotations;
relation types;
novel/background relation labels, if you actually use them.

This is methodology-specific and therefore worth stating even though Chapter 2 introduced the annotation schema.

You could potentially use a compact table if there are enough fields:

BioRED component	Use in this study
Abstract text	LLM input
Entity annotations	NER ground truth
Entity identifiers	NEN ground truth
Relation annotations	RE ground truth
Train split	Few-shot/development resources
Development split	EPI development
Test split	Final evaluation

That would be very effective.

Paragraph 4 — Preprocessing

Only include this if you actually performed meaningful preprocessing.

State things like:

how BioRED files were parsed;
conversion into your internal Python representation/dataframe;
whether titles + abstracts or abstracts alone were used;
whether annotations were transformed;
whether any documents/annotations were excluded;
preservation of original text/spans.

Don't invent a preprocessing stage just to have something to write about. If you basically parsed BioRED into Python objects and passed the text to the LLM, say exactly that.

What not to repeat

Avoid re-explaining:

why BioRED is broader than GAD/ChemProt;
why benchmark datasets are useful;
what its six entity/eight relation types mean;
why BioRED is suitable for KG construction;
limitations of benchmark evaluation;
distribution shift;
why contemporary evaluation is necessary.

You already spent an entire literature-review subsection establishing BioRED's characteristics and suitability.

If necessary, one sentence is enough:

BioRED was selected for the reasons outlined in Section 2.4.2.

Then move immediately into what you did with it.

The key distinction

2.4.2 BioRED:

What is this dataset, what does the literature say about it, and why is it appropriate?

3.2.1 BioRED Benchmark Corpus:

Which version/data did I use, how did I partition/process it, and what role did each component play in my experiment?

If you stick rigidly to that distinction, you should have almost no problematic repetition between the two chapters.
